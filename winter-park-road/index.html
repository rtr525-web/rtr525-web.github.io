Season 1 — Winter Park Landmarks

Verified historical stories from Winter Park, Florida — cross-posted across YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels, plus monthly deep-dive documentaries.

WPR #01 · 1981Real-Photo Remake

The 1981 Sinkhole That Became Lake Rose

In May 1981, a massive karst sinkhole opened on West Fairbanks Avenue in Winter Park, swallowing a three-bedroom house, five Porsches, a camper, and the deep end of the municipal Olympic pool — becoming Lake Rose.

WPR #02 · 1881Historic District

Hannibal Square — The West Side That Built Winter Park

Founded in 1881 for the Black families, carpenters, masons, and citrus workers who built Winter Park's railroads, streets, and estates — and whose votes in 1887 secured the town's charter.

WPR #03 · 1950Museum & Gardens

Albin Polasek — The Sculptor Who Worked With One Hand

After suffering a paralyzing stroke at age 70 upon retiring to Lake Osceola in Winter Park, Czech-American sculptor Albin Polasek carved and modeled over a dozen monumental works using only his right hand.

WPR #04 · 1933 / 2000Architecture

Casa Feliz — The 750-Ton House They Hauled Across Town

Designed by architect James Gamble Rogers II in 1933 using reclaimed 19th-century Andalusian-style brick, the Barbour estate was saved from demolition in 2000 by rolling all 750 tons down Interlachen Avenue.

WPR #05 · 1930sChain of Lakes

Genius Drive & The Peacocks of Wind Song

How industrialist Charles Hosmer Morse's granddaughter Jeannette Genius McKean turned a winding lakeside lane between Lake Mizell and Lake Virginia into a sanctuary for art and free-roaming peafowl.

WPR #06 · 1932Rollins College

Annie Russell Theatre — Florida's Historic Stage

Built in 1932 on the Rollins College campus in Spanish Colonial Revival style for legendary stage actress Annie Russell — the longest continuously operating theater in Florida.

WPR #07 · 1938Lake Maitland

Kraft Azalea Garden — The Exedra on Lake Maitland

Opened in 1938 on a shaded 5.2-acre peninsula jutting into Lake Maitland, anchored by giant bald cypresses and the neoclassical marble Exedra monument donated by Judge Leonard Wood.

WPR #08 · 1940Botanical History

Theodore Mead's 5,000 Orchids — Mead Botanical Garden

Naturalist Theodore L. Mead spent five decades hybridizing orchids, caladiums, and amaryllis in Oviedo and Winter Park before his friends established the 47-acre Mead Botanical Garden in 1940.

WPR #09 · 1938Chain of Lakes

The Scenic Boat Tour — Running the Logging Canals Since 1938

Captain Walt Meloon launched the Winter Park Scenic Boat Tour in 1938, guiding open pontoon boats through the narrow brick-walled 19th-century sawdust-and-timber logging canals connecting Lakes Osceola, Virginia, and Maitland.

WPR #10 · 1942Park Avenue

The Morse Museum's Tiffany Treasure

When Louis Comfort Tiffany's Long Island estate Laurelton Hall burned in 1957, Hugh and Jeannette McKean rescued its surviving leaded-glass windows, lamps, and the entire 1893 World's Columbian Exposition chapel for Winter Park.

Park Avenue — Winter Park's Brick Living Room

Laid out alongside the South Florida Railroad tracks in 1882 with an open central park on one side and brick storefronts on the other — how one tree-canopied street stayed the heart of Winter Park for 140 years.

Rollins College — Florida's First College & Mister Rogers' Alma Mater

Founded by New England Congregationalists in 1885 on the shores of Lake Virginia — where Fred Rogers graduated in 1951 with a degree in music composition and found the life philosophy behind Mister Rogers' Neighborhood.
