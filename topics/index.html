Fitness

  • Analysis Nov 6: a 2026 review of 60 studies in postmenopausal women — strength training’s strongest effects were on systolic and mean blood pressure

    Feeds: Built Not Born Fitness

    A Nov 6, 2026 analysis of a 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis in the Journal of Applied Physiology says the review examined 60 studies covering 1,931 postmenopausal women aged 52 to 78, isolating resistance training rather than combining it with aerobic exercise, in programmes typically running two to three sessions a week for eight to 24 weeks. It found improvements in blood pressure, resting heart rate, blood vessel function and autonomic regulation, with the strongest effects on systolic and mean blood pressure and smaller effects on diastolic pressure and resting heart rate. The analysis also carries the review’s own cautions: most included trials carried a higher risk of bias and none evaluated hard outcomes such as heart attacks or strokes.

    Why it matters: Marker improvements are not event reductions is the honest-evidence episode: the review supports an effect on pressure and vessel measures in a specific population, and its authors say plainly that the harder claim was not tested.
